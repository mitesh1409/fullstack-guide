## What is a DDoS Attack?

A **Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS)** attack is an attempt to overwhelm a server, network, or service with a flood of internet traffic — making it slow or completely unavailable to legitimate users.

The word *distributed* means the attack comes from **many sources at once** (often thousands of compromised devices called a **botnet**), making it much harder to block than a single-source attack.

**How it works:**
1. An attacker infects many devices (computers, IoT devices) with malware
2. These devices form a botnet under the attacker's control
3. On command, all devices simultaneously bombard the target with requests
4. The target gets overwhelmed and crashes or becomes unreachable

**Common types:**
- **Volumetric attacks** — flood bandwidth with massive traffic (e.g. UDP floods)
- **Protocol attacks** — exploit weaknesses in network protocols (e.g. SYN floods)
- **Application-layer attacks** — target web apps with seemingly legitimate requests (e.g. HTTP floods)

## How to Protect Against DDoS

**For individuals:**
- Keep all devices patched and updated (so they don't become botnet members)
- Use a reputable router with built-in firewall
- Avoid clicking suspicious links that could install botnet malware

**For websites & businesses:**

- **Use a CDN** (like Cloudflare or Akamai) — they absorb and distribute traffic globally, neutralizing volume attacks
- **Enable rate limiting** — restrict how many requests a single IP can make per second
- **Deploy a Web Application Firewall (WAF)** — filters malicious traffic before it hits your server
- **Use anycast network diffusion** — spreads attack traffic across multiple servers
- **Over-provision bandwidth** — having extra capacity buys time during an attack
- **Have an incident response plan** — know who to call and what to do when an attack starts
- **Use DDoS-specific mitigation services** — providers like Cloudflare, AWS Shield, or Radware specialize in this

**At the network level:**
- Configure **IP blacklisting/allowlisting**
- Use **traffic scrubbing centers** that filter malicious packets
- Enable **BGP blackholing** to drop attack traffic upstream

---

The most practical first step for most websites is simply enabling **Cloudflare's free tier** — it provides solid DDoS protection out of the box with minimal setup.
